# Investment Thesis
Perhaps, Nvidia’s (NVDA) latest earnings represented the point where the narrative around AI completely changed. For years, investors were thinking of Nvidia as primarily benefiting from hyperscaler AI spending and the growth in demand for its GPUs.

What these earnings quietly showed, however, is that Nvidia has evolved into the foundational infrastructure layer in the world of AI. It is not only selling the chips anymore. The company started to monetize the entire AI factory stack that involves compute, networking, orchestration, sovereign AI deployments and even CPUs. Since my last coverage, NVDA is up 8% but it still hasn’t experienced the kind of explosive rerating many smaller AI stocks have seen this year.

Considering that the annualized revenue run rate is reaching a staggering $400 billion, sovereign AI growth is already exceeding 80% and agentic AI is significantly boosting compute intensity per interaction, I think that there may be an element of misunderstanding how structurally big Nvidia’s opportunity truly becomes over the next decade.

# How Nvidia’s Q1 Earnings Quietly Changed The AI Narrative
If anything, Nvidia’s latest earnings showed that the company became something way more important than a provider of high-performance GPUs to hyperscalers. This time, Nvidia posted revenues up by 85% year over year to $82 billion with guidance for next quarter suggesting the company’s annualized revenue run rate reaching a whopping $364 billion ($91 billion*4).

It is important to note, though, that this earnings report has shown us quite a few other things. First off, the growth in the ACIE segment was much bigger than in hyperscaler revenue, where the latter came at $38 billion and the former at $37 billion. ACIE grew 31% over a quarter, whereas hyperscalers declined 14%. This quiet signals how much of a risk it might be to continue thinking of Nvidia as an undiversified vendor of chips dependent on hyperscaler demand. It shows that the company continues to grow thanks to sovereign AI initiatives and industrial use of its GPUs.

Secondly, the company’s CEO, Jensen Huang, highlighted quite a few times in the earnings call the importance of computing as a revenue-generation resource, talked about AI factories, tokens per dollar and lowest token generation cost. That is important since the logic behind infrastructure demand changes accordingly. Nvidia reported that the prices for its H100 rentals grew by 20% year-to-date, with A100 cloud pricing rising by 15%. This is remarkable, considering the fact that the company managed to boost the global capacity of AI compute by a massive amount.

Thirdly, Nvidia disclosed the incredible growth of its data center networking revenue, which surged by almost 3x compared to a year ago. In addition to that, InfiniBand revenue went up by more than 4x in comparison with last year's level. Finally, the company mentioned the fact that GB300 is capable of delivering up to a 2.7x increase in throughput along with reducing the cost per token by 60%.

All these figures are crucial since we start realizing that the expansion in AI spending has started to be mostly dependent on interconnect, networking and clustering abilities rather than merely computing capacity. This is reflected by Spectrum-X now becoming larger than all Ethernet peers combined. Nvidia is no longer monetizing just GPUs. It started to monetize the whole stack of technologies needed for building AI factories.

# The Vera CPU Announcement Might Be Far More Dangerous Than Most People Realize
For many people, the Vera CPU announcement was nothing but an expansion into yet another business area for Nvidia. What people do not get, however, is how this move could impact its competitors like AMD (AMD) and Intel (INTC). The CPU has always been the one control layer in AI infrastructure architecture not being monopolized by Nvidia.

All the AI clusters still required the usage of AMD and Intel CPUs in order to orchestrate, perform the tasks, store information, manage infrastructure, etc. Now that Nvidia is releasing a CPU, it means that this last layer of AI infrastructure architecture could also be captured by Nvidia and its monopoly extended to that sector.

During the earnings call, Jensen Huang admitted that Nvidia has clear visibility towards earning close to $20 billion in revenues from selling its CPUs. While that sounds impressive in terms of money, I find its strategic importance more valuable. By integrating CPUs into its existing stack of AI infrastructure technologies, Nvidia increases barriers to entry.

CUDA has always been considered Nvidia's key moat, with developers getting accustomed to working within this environment. Vera CPUs mean that Nvidia can take over not only the computation but also the orchestration and management of infrastructure. That is the reason why Nvidia could benefit from its enormous advantage in creating such infrastructure and controlling the ecosystem at every step. With the combination of GPUs, CPUs, networking, NVLink, software stack, inference optimization, orchestration capabilities and financing, Nvidia has made a very tough choice for the competitors to make.

# Why Nvidia Stalled While AI Stocks Exploded
NVDA’s market cap is now reaching $5 trillion with a forward P/S ratio close to 13.5x and a forward P/E at 24.5x based on FY2027 estimates. Yet, if you see how many AI stocks rallied lately and Nvidia stalled in comparison, it shows how the narrative is slowly shifting.

Investors have already rerated many smaller AI companies simply based on the potential that such stocks possess. Nvidia, in turn, has become a company providing revenues, not options. Annualized revenues of Nvidia are already exceeding $364 billion, which means that moving from here would require extraordinary efforts. That is why investors started to think of the long-term potential of Nvidia in comparison with shorter options that small caps provide.

Interestingly enough, consensus estimates of the revenues for FY2028 reach $540 billion with FY2028 forward P/E ratio estimated at just 17x despite the forecasted 40% EPS growth per year. If Nvidia succeeds in proving that sovereign AI, Vera CPUs, network expansion and agentic inference help increase TAM faster than expected, then the stock rerates based on its duration and scalability of revenues. Nvidia has to be treated more like an infrastructure coordination platform and not a chipmaker anymore.

# The Risk That Actually Matters

While Nvidia's rivalry with AMD or need for advanced custom chips could be a concern, the real risk could be found in its supply chain management issues. As of now, almost the entire industry of AI infrastructure depends on Nvidia due to its technology. Therefore, it needs advanced semiconductor manufacturing in the form of TSMC (TSM) and CoWoS packaging.

Geopolitical tensions regarding Taiwan or any other issues with TSMC could slow down Nvidia's growth even without any changes in demand coming. Thus, I believe that supply chain dependence is the only material risk at this point.

Ironically, Nvidia's success and its monopoly in AI infrastructure make this risk even greater. Almost everyone wants to standardize on Nvidia's products, which means that its infrastructure is in high demand, but it cannot increase production capacity fast enough. This is the biggest overlooked risk in the entire AI trade right now.

# Takeaway
Nvidia has become an operating system for global AI infrastructure via GPUs, networking, CPUs, sovereign AI deployments and agentic inference. If management manages to achieve all its objectives, then current valuations would not be viewed as extremely expensive anymore.
