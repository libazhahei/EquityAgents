# Very high revenue growth can sustain for longer
Nvidia is targeting the PC market more aggressively with its Vera CPUs, which are ramping up more from cloud partners in H2 CY26:


Building on the success of our Grace CPU, Vera is arriving just in time to meet this inflection... Vera will deliver up to 1.5x faster performance per core, 2x performance per watt and 4x density per rack compared to x86-based alternatives. Vera CPU opens a brand-new $200 billion TAM for NVIDIA, a market we have never addressed before, and every major hyperscale and system maker is partnering with us to get it deployed. We have visibility to nearly $20 billion in total CPU revenue this year, setting us up to become the world's leading CPU supplier.

- CFO Colette Kress in the Q1 FY27 earnings call.

To give you a sense of scale, the $20B in incremental CPU revenue this year amounts to an ~8% uplift to TTM revenues of $253B.

Another good sign is the fact that Foxconn's (FXCOF) monthly revenue trend is accelerating nicely to such a level that the May month this year has already reached the previous years' Sep-Nov's seasonal peak levels

The key driver of Foxconn's revenue acceleration here is strong momentum for its cloud and networking AI products. Overall, this is a positive leading indicator for Nvidia, as Foxconn (also called Hon Hai Precision Industry) sells more than 50% of the latter's AI input hardware components.

Due to these revenue tailwinds, I anticipate that Nvidia will continue growing its revenues at very high 85%+ rates for the next couple of quarters:

# Falls in GPU rental prices and a foray into the PC market can weigh down on gross margins
The GPU compute rental prices for H100 (the most mature GPU deployed widely across hyperscalers) and B200 (the newer, high-performance GPU) have been stagnant to falling:
This puts pressure on the companies leasing out GPUs, such as Oracle (ORCL), which some reports say is earning a fairly thin 14% margin in this business. I think falling GPU rental yields can eventually pressure Nvidia to make some concessions on the premium pricing for its AI chips. This can be a headwind to its high 75% gross margins.

Also, I note that Nvidia is foraying into the consumer PC market with the RTX Spark superchip. Now, the consumer PC business has high competition, is commoditized, and as a result has lower single-digit type margins. So revenue toward consumer hardware can dilute the company's overall gross margins profile.

For these reasons, I believe we may see some gross margin erosion ahead:

Ultimately, I view this as another medium- to longer-term risk for Nvidia's pricing power and future market share.

# NVDA stock looks undervalued on multiple measures

Dissecting the drivers of the stock, NVDA's earnings multiple has been rising rapidly, whilst its 1-yr fwd P/E valuation multiples have been contracting:
This means the stock's near-term fundamental outlook is still very robust. It is only being held back by the more sentiment-driven factor of the valuation multiple's ebbs and flows.

Relative to some semiconductor comps, NVDA stock is trading at a 28% discount to the 29.2x 1-yr fwd P/E median of its comps:
