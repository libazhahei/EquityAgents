# Investment Thesis

The AI-driven bull run in semiconductor stocks has hit pause over the past few weeks, with NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) shares dropping >10% from the ATH made in mid-April. The current weakness is, in my view, a potentially attractive accumulation opportunity, as Nvidia's commercial momentum continues to accelerate.

# NVIDIA's Fundamentals Show No Sign Of Slowing
To bring Nvidia's momentum into perspective, in Q1 FY 2027, the company generated $81.6 billion of revenue, up 85% YoY and 20% sequentially. Within that, Data Center revenue reached $75.2 billion, up 92% YoY and 21% sequentially. This matters because Nvidia is no longer compounding off a small base. It is adding more than $10 billion of sequential revenue in a single quarter while sustaining gross margins around 75%.

Even more importantly, management guided Q2 FY2027 revenue to $91 billion, plus or minus 2%, implying another double-digit sequential step-up. Notably, the guide also excludes China Data Center compute revenue, which means the underlying demand signal is strong enough to absorb a major export-restricted geography and still push the company toward a roughly $360 billion annualized revenue run-rate.

# with markets continuously discovering that Nvidia's AI infrastructure opportunity is larger than previously assumed
What I find more impressive is that Nvidia's commercial momentum is underwritten by a still-ongoing upward revision cycle. For context, over the past two years, FY 2027 revenue expectations have effectively doubled, with consensus revenue now around $393–395 billion. The consensus expectation has moved higher in repeated step-changes, especially around earnings events, suggesting that each reporting cycle is forcing the market to rebase Nvidia's future revenue power upward.

The key implication is that the market is continuously discovering that Nvidia's AI infrastructure opportunity is larger, more visible, and more concentrated in high-demand data center workloads than previously assumed. In my view, as long as revenue estimates keep moving higher, the stock can continue to find support even after a strong run.

Management commentary supports the same conclusion. Jensen Huang described the buildout of AI factories as “the largest infrastructure expansion in human history” and said it is accelerating at extraordinary speed. On the Q1 call, he went further, saying demand had “gone parabolic” because agentic AI is now doing productive work, tokens are becoming profitable, and model developers are racing to produce more compute. Furthermore, Nvidia's demand is no longer only about training larger models. The revenue pool is broadening toward inference, reasoning, agents, synthetic data, and robotics. That changes the buyer psychology: customers are not simply optimizing chip budgets but securing scarce productive capacity.

# Demand becomes not only stronger but also more “visible”
There is strong evidence of improving demand visibility, as the CAPEX cycle is extending beyond Blackwell into the next architecture generation. Management has said Vera Rubin should begin ramping in the second half of 2026, and CEO Jensen Huang added that he expects Nvidia to remain supply-constrained throughout Vera Rubin's entire life.

The OpenAI (OPENAI) partnership announced back in September 2025 reinforces this point. Nvidia and OpenAI announced a strategic plan to deploy at least 10 gigawatts of Nvidia systems, representing millions of GPUs, with the first gigawatt expected to come online in the second half of 2026 on Vera Rubin. Nvidia also intends to invest up to $100 billion in OpenAI progressively as each gigawatt is deployed. The scale is important: one gigawatt is already a massive AI infrastructure commitment; 10 gigawatts is a multi-year buildout program. It also directly links Nvidia's roadmap to one of the most aggressive frontier-model customers in the market.

Hyperscaler commentary points in the same direction. Microsoft Corporation (MSFT) said it added another gigawatt of capacity in the quarter and remains on track to double its data center footprint in two years, while continuing to modernize its fleet with the latest Nvidia and Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD) systems. Alphabet Inc. (GOOG, GOOGL) reported Q1 CapEx of $35.7 billion, largely directed toward AI technical infrastructure, while Google Cloud revenue grew 63% YoY to $20 billion, and backlog nearly doubled sequentially to $462 billion. More importantly, management raised 2026 CapEx guidance to $180–190 billion and said 2027 CapEx should increase significantly again.

Amazon.com, Inc. (AMZN) and Meta Platforms, Inc. (META) tell the same story. AWS revenue grew 28% YoY to $37.6 billion, its fastest growth in 15 quarters, while Amazon said higher property and equipment purchases were primarily driven by AI investments. The company also disclosed plans to deploy more than one million Nvidia GPUs starting in 2026, while Bedrock processed more tokens in Q1 than in all prior years combined, and customer spend rose 170% sequentially. Meta, meanwhile, raised its 2026 CapEx outlook to $125–145 billion, citing higher component pricing and additional data center costs to support future-year capacity.

The supply chain data supports the same conclusion. Nvidia disclosed $119 billion of manufacturing, supply, and capacity commitments as of April 2026, with $95 billion payable through the remainder of FY2027 and the balance extending into FY2028–FY2031. This is a hard financial signal that Nvidia is securing supply across both current and future architectures. TSMC has also continued to describe AI demand as difficult to satisfy.

Lastly, memory commentary further extends the visibility window. SK Hynix has said its 2026 DRAM, high-bandwidth memory (“HBM”), and NAND output is sold out, while Micron Technology, Inc. (MU) has indicated that HBM demand is already committed under multi-year agreements. Micron also sees the HBM market expanding from roughly $35 billion in 2025 to around $100 billion by 2028. This matters because HBM is one of the core constraints in AI accelerator systems. If memory supply is sold out, customers are signing multi-year agreements, and hyperscalers are still raising capex plans into 2027, then AI infrastructure buyers are not behaving as if this is a short-cycle inventory build. They are behaving as if compute will remain strategically scarce for several more years.

# Valuation still has upside
I value Nvidia using the residual earnings model: For Nvidia, I use EPS estimates aligned with analyst consensus through FY 2031, and for the terminal value, I apply a 9% cost of equity and 3% terminal growth. This gives me an implied target price of ~$265 per share.

# A note on risks
In my view, the main risk for Nvidia is not demand size or visibility today but the potential for this demand to be excessive vs. economics: if AI infrastructure capacity grows faster than monetization, hyperscaler CapEx could slow, and Nvidia's visibility would compress. At the same time, competition is another risk. Custom silicon, AMD, and other accelerators may not displace Nvidia, but they could pressure pricing and margins over time. Finally, expectations are already high. Any slowdown in estimate revisions, export tightening, or weaker AI ROI could pressure the multiple, even if Nvidia remains the clear market leader.

# Investor takeaway
My conclusion is that Nvidia's momentum has become more visible, not less visible, despite the enormous absolute scale of the business. The company has current-period acceleration, next-quarter guidance acceleration, customer capex acceleration, supply-chain pre-commitments, architecture-level visibility into Vera Rubin, and multi-year demand signals from frontier AI customers. The risk is not that demand is weak today.

The real debate is whether the industry overbuilds later. But based on the evidence available now, the market is still dealing with a system where demand is being pulled forward by profitable inference, agentic workloads, and enterprise adoption, while supply remains constrained across chips, memory, packaging, power, and data centers. That gives Nvidia unusually strong visibility through 2028. I rate Nvidia Buy with a ~$265 per share target price.
