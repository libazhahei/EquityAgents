# Summary
Nvidia Corporation faces mounting headwinds from an AI capex bubble, unsustainable demand drivers, and intensifying competition from hyperscalers’ in-house chips.
The AI capex market it serves faces at least 29 headwinds identified in this article.
NVDA’s revenue is highly concentrated, with 65% from four customers, many of whom are actively developing alternatives to Nvidia’s GPUs.
I maintain a short recommendation on NVDA, expecting AI capex and Nvidia-specific challenges to become evident by 2027, despite near-term strength from the Rubin GPU.

# Investment Thesis

“at the beginning of this year, an issue that never came up (people were totally happy with the amount they were spending) to, all of a sudden, a huge issue.”

Sam Altman June 2, 2026.
On December 15th, 2025, I published an article titled 17 Reasons To Short NVIDIA To $75. Since that time, I also published 22 Reasons AI Data Center Construction Is In A Bubble 22 Reasons AI Data Center Construction Is In A Bubble. I have actually been warning about this in Seeking Alpha since September 16th, 2025.

My reasons for shorting Nvidia Corporation (NVDA) and claiming AI capex is in a bubble have little to do with AI growth itself. I fully expect AI usage to continue to grow and to rapidly evolve to ever more useful purposes. But future AI usage will likely not require the massive data center construction now envisioned. In fact, that amount of construction may not even be possible for a number of reasons. NVDA not only has exposure to an AI capex bubble but is also facing rapidly increasing competition. That makes it more vulnerable than many other AI capex players.

Since my most recent article in February, so much has happened, giving NVDA many more reasons to hit a wall, which should be evident sometime in 2027. I am now up to 35 reasons from 17 in my prior article.

The first 29 reasons discuss why AI capex is a bubble that should start to deflate in 2027. The last 6 are specific to NVDA.

# 29 AI Capex Headwinds
Data center construction has grown so fast that it has run into many large and increasing headwinds. These are detailed below in no particular order.

1. Future AI will need less power – the backbone of current data centers is NVDA’s expensive, power-hungry chips. A different, less power-hungry approach was first indicated by Chinese company DeepSeek’s large language model (LLM), a year ago. DeepSeek (DEEPSEEK) proved you can build an LLM that’s almost as good without using the latest chips and data centers. There are other emerging technologies, such as Spiking Neural Networks, which use less than 10% of the power by turning off when not used. Looking further out, think about how little power the human brain uses. By many measures, AI has not yet approached the human brain, which uses way less power. It is highly likely we will find ways to run AI using hardware that more closely mimics the human brain.

2. Power constraints - The U.S. power grid is insufficient to supply all the expected new data centers. In fact, it is already straining the grid, and much more is planned or under construction. Currently, data centers requiring 150 gigawatts of power are planned. That is a 12% increase in the 1,300 gigawatt capacity of the U.S. While that may not seem like a lot, there was very little increase in capacity for decades until 2025. It takes years to bring new power plants online, sometimes longer than a decade. The expense involved is also massive. The likely short-term result is higher power bills for families and businesses. That is already leading to political pushback that will get only more intense. By the way, this does not offset Reason #1 above. This is an immediate problem, while #1 is longer-term.

3. Efficiency gains - Efficiency gains, some of which may be driven by AI itself, will reduce the need to invest in AI infrastructure. AI technology will eventually be able to create more AI using less power and data center capacity. In a Capitalist system, if there is money to be made by creating efficiencies, someone will do it.

4. Insufficient ROI – This, in my opinion, is the most important near-term reason. AI has already changed a lot of things, but how much of that has increased profits? In August 2025, MIT published a study showing that 95% of businesses were not getting adequate returns on their AI investment. A PWC Survey dated January 19, 2026, of 4,454 CEOs concluded that only 12% of CEOs say AI has delivered both cost and revenue benefits. Overall, 33% report gains in either cost or revenue, while 56% say they have seen no significant financial benefit to date. The survey didn’t measure how many of the 33% actually met ROI targets. So far, there are limited examples of market-rate returns for larger investments. The only ones clearly getting decent returns are certain Hyperscalers: Microsoft (MSFT), Alphabet (GOOG), and Amazon (AMZN). Their cloud growth has started to accelerate a little after years of slowing growth. But those returns are not yet sufficient to justify the massive investment. CTOs, unchecked by CFOs are currently pushing hard into AI. This can’t last, and the CFOs will start pushing back. A big risk here is that the Hyperscalers’ customers, who are flocking to them now, become discouraged when their CFOs discover they are not getting sufficient returns.

5. Inefficient AI Engineering – A recent Yahoo Finance article estimated that up to 82% of AI spending is lost due to bugs, rewrites, and delays. This was based on a survey of 2444 companies. This ties into the ROI issue in Reason #4. Companies are finding that Agentic AI is a lot more expensive than they realized. This could eventually cause quite a pullback.

6. They Are Giving AI Away – Much of current AI use is driven by heavy subsidies or even free access. Most consumers using LLMs are not paying for them. Hyperscalers are being paid sufficiently, but not the LLMs, who have to pay the Hyperscalers. Many businesses using AI are paying rates that are unprofitable to the providers. SpaceX's (SPCX) recent S-1 filing indicated that Anthropic is receiving massive discounts on infrastructure rates during specific deployment months. Are you paying anything for your use of ChatGPT or Gemini? If not, they are losing money every time you have a query. Who is paying? The investors and suppliers, such as NVDA, through circular financing, are (more on that below). It’s hard to tell how much real demand there is when it’s heavily discounted or free. What will happen when users have to pay the full price?

7. Shareholder and CFO patience - How long will finance departments, directors, and investors allow this massive investment with little measurable return? Investors are already balking. Late last year, the stocks of both Meta Platforms (META) and Oracle (ORCL) dropped 11% the day after announcing additional capex spending. On February 5, 2026, and the following day, Amazon’s (AMZN) )stock dropped 10% after announcing a massive increase in data center spending. The Hyperscalers’ (Amazon, Microsoft (MSFT), Alphabet (GOOG), Meta, and OpenAI (OPENAI) executives must be increasingly aware of these reactions.

8. NIMBY pushback - Since my prior article, just 4 months ago, this has become a dramatically bigger problem. There is increasing pushback against data centers and difficulty getting them powered. Data centers are loud, can increase household energy bills, and add few new permanent jobs. A number of projects have already been rejected, forcing data center construction increasingly to more rural areas.

9. New technology often takes longer than you think – This should be looked at along with Reason #4 (insufficient ROI). The example of autonomous driving shows that this type of technology takes longer than expected to work well for more complex tasks. The key here is to work well. Autonomous driving uses AI. Elon Musk and others have been promising an autonomous car for a decade. Waymo by Alphabet appears to be finally getting there, but it took a long time. Tesla is still not there. Another example is the internet itself. The internet turned out to be huge, but it took longer than most investors thought. Hundreds of companies went bankrupt first.

10. Unsolved challenges – This is the biggest reason for Reasons #4 and #9 outside of the Hyperscalers. Key challenges to achieving a market-rate AI ROI include poor data quality, talent shortages, unclear business cases, bugs, rewrites, and difficulty integrating AI into legacy systems. The next phase of AI investment is converting the raw power of the data centers into something that works. That involves mapping, which requires talented experts. It’s going to take time to develop sufficient experts to make it work. AI will really struggle with mapping without expert humans because most business processes have numerous steps, decision points, and alternatives.

11. Job pushback – Do you expect local, State, and Federal governments to idly stand by as their voters start losing lots of jobs to AI? There will be pressure to do something if that happens. On the other hand, if it doesn’t happen… then AI will not provide an acceptable ROI. Anthropic (ANTHRO) CEO Dario Amodei recently said "the idea that we could have 5 or 10% GDP growth, but also, you know, 10% unemployment, it's not logically inconsistent at all.” Dario, that won’t go over well.

12. Commoditization – There is only so much data available for digestion and analysis. Generative AI improvements (think LLMs) going forward will become increasingly limited while requiring more and more power and GPUs. The applicable term here is commoditization. Commodities don’t bring big profit margins. Only a small fraction of users currently pay for LLMs. Further, many corporate functions moving to AI only need to be fully trained once, with much smaller tweaks later.

13. Capex approaching cash flow – This one is a real problem. As shown below, the Hyperscalers plan to spend all of their 2026 cash flow on data center capex. This is from an industry that has traditionally thrown off huge free cash flow. The one with the biggest plans is OpenAI, which has no cash flow even before capex and is reliant on investors for its spending. OpenAI’s LLM appears to have lost its lead over Google. The Hyperscalers will have to leverage their balance sheets or dilute in order to spend more than they plan this year. That means they may be getting close to the limit of what they can or will be willing to spend. Alphabet recently diluted its shareholders to raise another $85 billion to pay for all of this. So it appears they are willing to go beyond existing cash flow. But how far?

14. CEOs Skeptical - The CEOs of the Hyperscalers themselves have all recently made comments expressing skepticism about AI Capex growth. For the quotes, see my prior article titled Hyperscalers Are Starting To Question Massive AI Spending Hyperscalers Are Starting To Question Massive AI Spending.

15. Massive spending moves the peak forward – If AI does not deliver the expected returns, the massive investment now will lead to a huge drop-off, as there will be way too much excess capacity. It will be like all the unused fiber laid down in the internet boom. The more spent now, the bigger the drop-off.

16. Circular financing – To sustain its rapid growth rate, NVDA (the largest beneficiary of data center construction) has engaged in significant circular financing over the past year. This means they give their customers money (through loans or investments) to buy their products. These include data center owners CoreWeave (CRWV), Applied Digital (APLD), Nscale, and Nebius (NBIS). It also includes OpenAI. Their main rival AMD has also used unusual financing, giving their largest customers AMD stock in return for their business.

17. Excess depreciation - Most of the large data center costs are capitalized. That will significantly reduce Hyperscaler profits in future years as depreciation on all those new assets kicks in. The Hyperscalers have addressed this by extending their depreciation periods. They now depreciate their NVDA GPUs over a 5 to 6-year period. This is a significant increase from the 3 to 4-year depreciation that was standard just a few years ago. If your chips depreciate faster than accounting depreciation, it will make the Hyperscalers much more cautious about buying the latest model in the future. Currently, the latest GPUs (Blackwells) are appreciating in value due to demand, while earlier models are rapidly depreciating.

18. Concentration - Four customers, likely Google, Meta, Amazon and Microsoft, were 65% of NVDA’s revenues last quarter. They are building most of the data centers along with OpenAI. If one or two of them back off, it could almost entirely stop data center growth or even decrease it.

19. High memory prices – You just need to look at the stock prices of Micron (MU), SanDisk (SNDK), Seagate (STX), and Western Digital (WDC) to know that memory prices have gone through the roof and are expected to stay there all year. This makes future data center construction even more expensive than in the past.

20. Lack of use cases – I think this one is critical longer term. Most future AI uses are unlikely to require the latest GPU. These uses include most robots and autonomous vehicles, and corporate functions such as accounting, HR, clerical, legal, and supply chain. They will reach a point of diminishing returns, where the current level is good enough. Medical research and videos are a clear exception.

21. Chip competition – Competition drives down costs. GPUs are the most expensive portion of data center construction. NVDA has had the data center GPU market to itself until now. AMD will be releasing a GPU (the MI400 series) later this year that comes close in many ways to NVDA’s latest, the Rubin GPU, being released at the same time. Meanwhile, Google has chips called TPUs that already drive its Gemini LLM and are increasingly competitive, as is Amazon with its Trainium chips. All three of these are a lot cheaper than NVDAs GPUs. AMD has already lined up way more business for its next GPU than for its current. We are not too far off from price wars.

22. PWC survey 1/19/26 - This survey of CEOs concluded that only 12% of CEOs say AI has delivered both cost and revenue benefits. Overall, 33% report gains in either cost or revenue, while 56% say they have seen no significant financial benefit to date.

23. Parallels To The Internet Bubble – There are many parallels to the 2000 Internet bubble burst. These include a massive amount of IPOs, construction, and hype just before the burst. The construction back then was primarily optical fiber, most of which went unused for years. There was also a huge run-up in the prices of almost every company involved. In the end, the internet proved to be a huge advancement. But not before the vast majority of companies building it either went under or suffered from huge stock price drops. We are about to have 3 historically huge IPOs. The chart below shows that the market historically doesn’t perform well after a surge in IPO dollar amounts.

24. Temporary Demand is Juicing AI Usage and Revenues – Much of current AI usage is the result of a land-grab phase by LLMs to train their models. This is not sustainable, as LLMs will become commoditized and the focus shifts toward inference. There is also something known as Tokenmaxxing. This is a new term where companies such as Amazon, Meta and Uber (UBER) created a user board for their employees who used the most tokens (an AI unit). The purpose was to get them to use AI more, so using AI to use AI. The result was massive data center usage. This is now reversing, and the leaderboards are being taken down. Another temporary surge is from bug detection. Tokenmaxxing, training, benchmarking and bug detecting are NOT AI uses. This surge in getting AI out there is distorting what actual AI usage is.

25. Too Much Unneeded Capacity – To give you an idea, Microsoft Excel is on nearly every PC in the world, yet people very rarely use even 1/100th of its capabilities. This hasn’t been a problem because Excel uses very little power compared to AI. It is a problem for AI. Companies need to become much more efficient in their use of AI.

26. Dead Internet Theory – This was originally considered a conspiracy theory, but now appears to have come true. Launchready this week said Cloudflare Radar shows bots are now 57.5% Cloudflare Radar shows bots are now 57.5% of HTTP requests to HLML pages. The majority of online content, searches, and social media interactions are now generated and consumed by bots and algorithms rather than real people. This is problematic because it reduces human trust in social media, search engines, online content, and ultimately AI. Many platforms are now populated by vast networks of automated accounts that "like," comment, and interact with each other in endless loops of synthetic activity. This one is scary.

27. Blown AI Budgets – Many companies' employees are blowing through their AI budgets for the year in months. Uber blew through their full year budget in 4 months and is cutting back. Microsoft reportedly canceled most of its internal Claude Code licenses, partly due to cost, six months after rolling them out.

28. 85% of AI revenues are still internal – A study released this week by TS Lombard and Bloomberg concluded that 85% of AI revenues come from “capex recycling.” That is a circular flow where AI companies spend on infrastructure, going to other companies in the same ecosystem. If true, this indicates AI is a long way from proving itself financially capable of justifying the massive amounts of investment.

29. Increased regulation - As Hyperscalers and LLMs become increasingly powerful and omnipresent, harsher regulations will follow. It’s already happening overseas. Heavily regulated companies grow more slowly and have compressed P/E ratios. Think banks, hospitals, and utilities.

# NVDA-Specific Issues
The following Nvidia-specific issues are in addition to the industry headwinds noted above.

Competition emerging - There is competition emerging beyond NVDA’s traditional competitors, such as Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) and Intel (INTC). The scary part is that it is from Nvidia’s largest customers themselves. Most of them now have on their own chips. Google has a very advanced one called a Tensor Processing Unit (TPU). It is more power-efficient than Nvidia’s GPUs. Google and Meta just announced they are working on a competing product. Not good when two of your largest customers are working together on an alternative to your GPU. Amazon has its Trainium. Microsoft’s is called Maia 200. Even Apple has one. On April 20, 2026, Anthropic agreed to use 1 million Trainium chips. These are all rapidly being adopted in the place of NVDA GPUs. The largest GPU buyers are quickly pivoting to using NVDA GPUs for only the most advanced training and their own chips for everything else.

Competition is Cheaper and More Efficient – All of the Hyperscaler’s own chips can perform all but the most advanced tasks for less cost. Startups that can't afford NVDA’s pricing are moving to Amazon Trainium. By offering these chips at a discount, Amazon is locking in the next generation of AI companies before they ever buy their first NVDA chip.

NVDA’s massive profit margin is unsustainable - NVDA last quarter had an operating profit margin of 65%. That is almost unheard of for hardware, except for Micron (also unsustainable). This is due to increased competition, even if you think AI will continue to rapidly grow. They are getting around it so far through circular deals and unsustainable demand driven by temporary situations (see Reason 24). Competition alone should lead to margin compression. Compression is likely when the training phase ends, and companies start to reduce third party token overuse.

Concentration of Customers – Last quarter, just 3 customers represented 64% of NVDA’s receivables. If even one backs off, it could cause quite a drop in revenues.

Temporary Competitor Demand – This is related to Reason #24. Apple, Google, Microsoft, and Amazon are using NVDA chips to train their in-house chips to replace NVDA GPUs!

Inference leads away from NVDA – NVDA chips are clearly the best for training. But AI is moving toward inference, and the Hyperscalers’ chips are geared for inference and are much cheaper than NVDA’s. Anthropic is currently negotiating to use it in Azure.

# NVDA Positives
NVDA wouldn’t be the most valuable company in the world if it didn’t have major strengths.

Ahead of Competitors - Nvidia’s GPUs are the second best-selling product in the world after Apple’s iPhone. While competition is rapidly emerging, NVDA still has a lead in its GPUs when you also factor in the software.

Rapid sales growth – Revenue growth for LLMs is surging. Growth for the AWS, Azure, and Google clouds has reversed a slowing trend and is now accelerating.

Jobs are being eliminated by AI– The jobs market clearly shows an increasing trend of job losses or lack of hiring tied to AI. Currently, this is mostly at IT firms, as their workflows are easiest to transfer to AI and they have more staff who understand how to do it.

Very rapid technological progress – In just the past 3 years since ChatGPT emerged, AI has advanced at a very rapid pace.

Jevons paradox – Like Moore’s Law, this is the theory that the more powerful AI gets, the more uses will be found, and it will grow to the sky as semiconductors did. The problem here is that most AI uses don’t require the latest, most expensive GPU.

Diversification – NVDA has announced new adjacent products such as CPUs.

# Take Away
AI investment, like most other investments, at the end of the day, is driven by capitalism. Investment occurs in anticipation of an adequate return, typically in the form of profits or dividends. The returns at this point are nowhere near justifying the investments made and, in my opinion, are unlikely ever to do so. I believe that the current AI surge is driven in part by several unsustainable factors such as Tokenmaxxing, training, and a manic land rush by the LLMs and Hyperscalers.

NVDA specifically is facing a double whammy of increased competition and a market facing massive headwinds. I listed 29 headwinds in this article. It is also reliant on a few large customers, most of whom are investing billions, often with NVDA chips, to move away from relying on NVDA. Investors in NVDA are mistaking a land rush for sustainable growth.

I continue to recommend a short position in NVDA. The new Rubin GPU should do well this year. Sometime in 2027, it should be clear that the headwinds I mentioned in AI capex, and NVDA specifically, are too much to sustain continued growth in NVDA GPU revenues.
