# Nvidia: Downgrade To Hold As Earnings Fail To Push Price Higher
Jun 06, 2026, 6:21 AM ETNVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) Stock, NVDA:CA Stock, ZNVD:CA Stock
# Summary
Nvidia is downgraded to hold as rapid price appreciation appears exhausted and market expectations are extremely elevated.
Despite stellar Q1 FY27 results—85% revenue growth and 100%+ EPS growth—NVDA's muted stock reaction signals a high bar for further upside.
Potential delays in US data center buildouts threaten consensus earnings growth forecasts for FY27 and FY28, challenging current valuation assumptions.
At 21x forward P/E, NVDA appears cheap if growth materializes, but execution risks from infrastructure bottlenecks warrant caution and a range-bound outlook.
# Introduction
Nvidia (NVDA) has been a great pick for a long time. Since I upgraded to strong buy in June 2025, the stock has delivered a return of 47% compared to the market return of 25%. Today, however, I believe the stock has likely seen the rapid price appreciation phase as in the rear-view mirror, at least for now. The company reported stellar earnings recently, to which the stock did absolutely nothing. This suggests to me the bar Nvidia now has to clear in order to see its stock price move meaningfully is very high indeed. This is partly a function of size; at $5tn in market cap, even a $50bn flow into the stock would only produce a 1% move.

The stock looks cheap on a forward P/E basis, but I think earnings being baked in are potentially at risk due to documented delays in the planned data center build-out. A clogging up of the data center pipeline could cause customers to meaningfully slow down their purchases and installations of Nvidia chips. I don't think this would lead to a decline in earnings, but it does make the current consensus estimates look quite challenged. Markets are expecting Nvidia to grow EPS by close to 90% in FY27 and another 40% in FY28.

From a technical perspective, the stock has put in a very neat head and shoulders pattern, which often signals a reversal of a prior trend. Nvidia had broken out of its price range as we came into earnings; however, the muted reaction suggests to me it might drift back down to its prior range.

I'm struggling to see the stock price moving meaningfully from here without a catalyst, and if strong earnings and a stellar keynote by Jensen at Computex failed to be that catalyst, I'm not sure what more the company can do in the near term. With all this considered, I am downgrading Nvidia to a hold as I expect it to be range bound for a while.

# Earnings
Nvidia reported its Q1 FY27 earnings on May 20th. Simply put, the numbers delivered against a very high set of analyst expectations. Revenue of $81.6bn grew by 85%, and EPS of $1.87 represented over 100% growth. The company highlighted Blackwell deployments across every major cloud player; Vera Rubin chips are on track for Q3 of this year with significant performance improvement over Blackwell, as highlighted by Jensen at Computex in Taiwan. Despite all of this super performance, the stock fell nearly 2% on the day, and it has moved lower since.

It appears to me that the stock has priced in so much good news that even stellar earnings and a furious pace of server releases are not enough to excite this market anymore. The reaction to Nvidia was at least relatively muted; it stood out from the reaction to Broadcom (AVGO) which delivered a beat to estimates and more than 50% EPS growth, yet the stock was hammered by unimpressed markets, sending it down more than 12% on the day.

Expectations have gotten very high, and analysts are putting out aggressive forecasts. If we go back in time to 2023, we saw analyst estimates trailing Nvidia's actual earning potential by a wide margin; hence, when the company beat on numbers, that stock moved higher aggressively as the market priced in this greater potential. The magnitude of beats has gotten smaller and smaller, meaning that solid earnings are not delivering as they used to.

# Data Center Delays
The fear I have is markets might have flipped and are now potentially overestimating Nvidia's earnings. Through no fault of Nvidia, the ability of their customers to take delivery of chips might be bottlenecked, if not already, then in the near future. A recent Bloomberg report noted that somewhere between a third and half of US data centers planned for 2026 are likely to be delayed or outright canceled. The report noted that of an estimated 12-16 GW of planned capacity, only 5 GW was currently under construction, with many projects having made zero progress.

So delays are cropping up in 2026, I wonder what this means for the projects planned in 2027 and 2028 which are significantly greater in volume than 2026. If a backlog develops in 2026 that naturally will spiral into 2027. If 50% of projects get delayed in 2026, how many of the original 2027 projects end up delayed as a knock-on effect? Could it be 70-80%?

Time will tell what the exact figure is, but the key point from my perspective is Nvidia may face a halt to its ferocious pace of chip orders and deliveries. Remember, the company is slated to grow earnings at 90% this year and 40% again next year. If their end customers don't have the data centers built or on time, this could push back chip deliveries and ultimately impact earnings growth. As I said, I think Wall Street might actually be overestimating earnings for Nvidia if these data center delays cannot be resolved.

The causes of the delays are not easy, quick fix items either, which is concerning. In a recent article, it was reported that interviews with industry execs highlighted a chronic shortage of labor, power, and equipment. In particular, the execs stated a lack of skilled tradespeople like electricians and huge bottlenecks in power generation were chief causes of delays. These are not simple problems to fix; skilled tradespeople are finite, and training up new people takes time.

On the power side, not only is availability a concern but also the impact of data centers crowding out individuals and the political backlash that entails. Polls in states like Virginia have shed light on a disgruntled public and turned data centers into a hot-button issue. A poll in March from Gallup even showed the public would rather live beside a nuclear power plant than a data center, not a good look for those seeking to throw up data centers at a rapid clip.

# Valuation
The valuation of Nvidia looks great if the expected earnings come to pass. The stock trades on a 21x P/E on the basis that it will deliver 80% EPS growth in 2026, and maybe it will. The supply of chips for 2026 is likely already sold out, so the stock is cheap on 2026 metrics. The concern, as I have noted above, is what about the growth trajectory after that. The market will always try to price stocks based upon their earnings potential over multiple years rather than just the year ahead.

The concern for me, as I noted above, is the scope for meaningful data center delays and what it might mean for future earnings. My sense is analysts have been building their earnings models based on the assumption that announced data centers will be built and built on time. If that fails to happen, then those models will overestimate the earnings for Nvidia, which could make today's valuation not a fair reflection of what we are paying for a true unit of earnings in this firm.

# Risks
The risks to my thesis could be either up or down; such is the nature of a hold recommendation. On the downside, let's suppose I am right about data center delays. If the delays prove to be incredibly significant, then Nvidia could see an air pocket of demand. I remember a few years ago analysts had talked about a demand air pocket as they thought Nvidia customers might take a pause to integrate the chips they had already bought before they committed to new orders. The opposite happened; they were voracious for more chips, and to facilitate those chip purchases, they embarked on vast build-outs of data centers.

However, what if today those data centers simply cannot be built in time and the firm simply cannot commit to buying more chips? This time round, it indeed has to take a pause and digest the chips it has already bought, as the speed of purchasing slows to match the speed of implementation. A sharp decline in chip sales is not my base case, and such a scenario represents a risk to my hold recommendation.

The flip side is, what if I have turned incrementally bearish at a time when Nvidia is trading at a screamingly cheap 21x valuation, and even if the stock earnings just simply do what they are expected to do and no more? The stock could reasonably re-rate back up to say 30x the same earnings; the stock would gain close to a third, and I would have switched to hold at the wrong time. My base case, given Nvidia's lack of luster post-earnings, is the stock will drift back into its prior trading range, as despite the low valuation, the markets showed little appetite to buy the stock despite some really robust numbers.

# Conclusion
After careful consideration and contemplation, I have decided to drop my rating on Nvidia back down to a hold. I had been bullish on the stock over the past number of years, and that call has done well for investors. As I see it right now, the stock is facing headwinds of its huge market cap and very elevated investor expectations. I was genuinely impressed with the firm's Q1 earnings numbers, and I thought Jensen's performance at Computex was powerful; this company is really delivering on a ferocious product roadmap.

The near-term looks challenged, though. The market was not impressed with earnings or the specs of Vera Rubin or RTX Spark announced in Taiwan. Nvidia, like all other companies, relies upon its customers willingness and ability to buy from it. There remains no doubt in my mind about customer willingness, but the ability of customers to reasonably continue the pace of GPU purchases looks challenged in light of delays to data center projects. Some customers might be happy to purchase chips and leave them sitting dormant while data centers get built, but I wouldn't expect that to be the default approach, as it is an inefficient use of capital. I still love the company, but I'm struggling with this moment as a setup; as a result, I rate Nvidia now as a hold.
