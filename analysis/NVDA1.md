# Investment Thesis

NVIDIA (NVDA) remains a Strong Buy despite a recent pullback in semiconductor stocks. AI demand continues to accelerate, and the company’s latest earnings and guidance reinforce my view that Nvidia remains one of the best-positioned beneficiaries of the ongoing AI infrastructure buildout. Since my last report, Nvidia stock has gained 13.3%, outperforming the S&P 500’s 8.5% return, while also exceeding my base-case price target of $235.55, which implied roughly 32% upside at the time.

While some investors have become cautious following the strong rally in AI and semiconductor stocks, I believe the market continues to underestimate both the duration and scale of AI-driven spending. The combination of expanding hyperscaler investments, increasing AI adoption, and Nvidia’s dominant position in accelerated computing provides a strong foundation for continued growth.

As a result, I maintain my Strong Buy rating on Nvidia. In this report, I analyze the company’s latest earnings and guidance, discuss the outlook for AI infrastructure spending, and update my price target.

# Nvidia Aligns Reporting Segment With AI Growth Focus
The importance of artificial intelligence to Nvidia’s business is clear. However, the company has now also embedded that in its reporting structure, which to me highlights that the company expects AI to be a longer-term growth story. The company has shifted from a gaming-heavy business to an AI-heavy business.

The new reporting structure looks as follows:

Data Center
Hyperscale
AI Clouds, Industrial & Enterprise, or ACIE
Edge Computing
It is a break from the company’s prior structure that included Data Center, Gaming, Professional Visualization, Auto, OEM & Other as market segments. The Data Center segment is the AI-heavy segment with Hyperscale, including revenues from public cloud and large internet companies, while ACIE includes purpose-built data centers and AI factors across industries and countries and highlights sovereign AI.

Edge computing includes most of the prior business outside of the Data Center reporting segment and includes the following:

Agentic AI
Physical AI
PCs
Game consoles
Workstations
Base Stations
Robotics
Automotive
# Nvidia AI Growth Continues To Accelerate
Total revenues grew 85% year-over-year and 20% sequentially to $81.6 billion, exceeding estimates by $2.5 billion. Data center sales grew 92% year-on-year and 21% sequentially to $75.2 billion, with 12% sequential growth and 115% year-on-year growth in Hyperscale revenues, and ACIE reported 31% sequential growth and 74% year-on-year growth to $37.4 billion. Edge computing sales grew 10% sequentially and 29% year-on-year to $6.4 billion.

AI demand continues to accelerate with the third consecutive quarter of acceleration in year-on-year growth and the fourteenth quarter of sequential growth. The Blackwell ramp-up is exceeding expectations as Nvidia executes the fastest ramp-up in history to at least somewhat keep up with demand. Previously there still had been the question for some customers whether the company would need the Hopper series for their data centers or the Blackwell series from a cost efficiency and capacity perspective. Naturally, with the Blackwell program ramping up, the Hopper series is gradually phased out, but with demand as robust as we see now, Blackwell is the go-to pick. Furthermore, we note that Hyperscale is not the highest growth area, as we saw ACIE showing nearly triple the sequential growth compared to Hyperscale. It somewhat reduces concerns on Hyperscale growth as we see momentum in AI cloud and sovereign AI as well, and this is a big business for Nvidia, accounting for half of the data center sales, and I believe that AI Clouds could ultimately be the bigger segment within the Data Center group. On Hyperscale, the company is adding Anthropic as a major customer, adding to the growth prospects. Also important to note is that Nvidia is entering the CPU market with their Vera CPU, widening their moat, and networking revenues tripled year-on-year to $15 billion.

In the edge computing business, growth drivers include physical AI with $9 billion in revenues over the past 12 months and robotaxi expansion. Consumer demand was weaker, but that is to be expected with high memory and system prices these days. Consumers are no longer Nvidia’s bread and butter, so it is not a major issue, but I looked for a refresh for my 6-year-old PC, and the price today would be 80%-120% higher than it would be six years ago, and that shows how unaffordable PC refreshes have become for the consumer.
# Nvidia Becomes More Shareholder Return Oriented

For the second quarter, the company expects $91 billion in revenues, indicating 11% sequential growth with gross margins stable at 75% and 9% growth in operating expenses. The company also continues to assume no sales to China.

Key growth drivers are not just the hyperscale growth, but we also note that sovereign AI, agentic AI, and physical AI will drive demand. Nvidia is also attempting to capture a bigger share of the AI infrastructure pie with its Vera CPU and networking solutions. Nvidia is increasingly transforming from a GPU to a broader AI infrastructure solutions provider.

That AI infrastructure pie is growing, as became clear during the earnings call:

With analysts now forecasting hyperscale CapEx to exceed $1 trillion in 2027 and agentic AI beginning to proliferate all industries, AI infrastructure spending is on track to reach $3 trillion to $4 trillion annually by the end of this decade.

On the topic of shareholder returns, Nvidia has increased its buyback authorization by $80 billion, bringing the total available authorization to $119 billion. Furthermore, the company increased its dividend from $0.01 to $0.25 and targets to return 50% of free cash flow to shareholders. It is a major shift from a pure growth company toward a growth-plus-shareholder returns company.

# Nvidia Upside Remains Strong as Earnings Expectations Rise Even Further
To determine multi-year price targets, The Aerospace Forum has developed a stock screener that uses a combination of analyst consensus on EBITDA, cash flows, and the most recent balance sheet data. Each quarter, we revisit those assumptions and update the stock price targets accordingly. In a separate blog, I have detailed our analysis methodology.

For Nvidia, I believe that there is a 59% upside to the stock price with a $318.82 price target, and that is arguably a conservative target driven by current year earnings against the peer group valuation, which is how Nvidia stock is also currently valued. If we were to pull in the FY28 earnings as I typically look at earnings one year ahead, we would get a $467.96 price target, indicating more than 130% upside.

Incorporated in my new price targets are:

The increased dividend as part of the 50% return of free cash flow to shareholders.
An 11% increase in EBITDA estimates for 2027-2029 with growth expectations increased by 7% this year, 18% next year, and 11% for 2029.
A 20% increase in free cash flow estimates for 2027-2029 with growth expectations increased by 19% this year, 21% next year, and 27% for 2029.

My EBITDA margin projections are currently expecting growth to be around 65%-68%, which is actually a slight reduction from the 72% I modeled earlier, driven by continued and accelerated investments in new product development. Cash conversion ratios are remaining strong as capital expenditures remain modest and the business continues to grow. Shareholder returns show that yield on cost is not going to look great for the years to come, but this is a stock that has substantial growth ahead, so it is not surprising to see yield on cost being poor. While substantially increasing shareholder purchases in the years ahead, we also see that the company will grow its net cash position. So, over time, I actually also hope to see Nvidia strategically deploying more than the 50% free cash flow target for shareholder returns.

# Conclusion: Nvidia Growth Keeps Impressing
Nvidia is becoming more and more of an AI growth story with upward pressure, and what is particularly notable is that the company has altered its reporting structure to reflect the business’ growth areas, and we are also seeing strong sovereign growth. Whereas this was mostly a hyperscale growth story and sales to China were happening or not happening, we are now seeing hyperscale growth, sovereign growth, and the third pillar of growth is likely going to be in the edge computing part of the business with agentic and physical AI growth. With Nvidia’s stock having recently sold off just like that of peers while expectations have actually improved, I maintain my strong buy rating.
