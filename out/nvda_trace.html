# NVDA Market Consensus Report

## Executive Summary

NVIDIA remains one of the most strongly covered large-cap names in the semiconductor space, with a consensus framework that is still anchored in extraordinary AI infrastructure demand. The current market view is broadly constructive: analysts expect FY2026 revenue around **$200B-$210B**, EPS around **$4.3-$4.4**, and record free cash flow near **$96.6B**. The most important consensus point is that NVIDIA’s **Data Center** business continues to dominate the earnings model, with FY2026 revenue consensus around **$181.9B** and gross margin expectations in the **mid-70% range**.

That said, the debate has shifted from “can NVIDIA grow?” to “how durable is the growth path?” The market is still underwriting a strong multi-year expansion in AI accelerator demand, but recent revisions show slightly more caution around Data Center revenue and margin assumptions. Valuation remains a central issue: even though forward multiples compress materially under long-term growth assumptions, the stock still screens as demanding in absolute terms.

**Coverage confidence:** high.  
**Analyst count:** 18 FY2026 EPS estimates (public proxy consensus).  
**Overall consensus score:** 0.9.

---

## 1) Quantitative Estimates

Consensus estimates remain very elevated and internally consistent with a high-growth AI hardware cycle.

- **FY2026 revenue:** around **$200B-$210B**
- **FY2026 EPS:** around **$4.3-$4.4**
- **FY2026 free cash flow:** record near **$96.6B**
- **FY2026 Data Center revenue:** around **$181.9B**
- **Gross margin:** expected in the **mid-70%s**

MarketBeat’s public estimate set shows **18 FY2026 EPS estimates**, with a **low of $4.03**, **high of $4.77**, and **average of $4.42**. Visible Alpha / S&P Global public commentary also places FY2026 Data Center revenue at **$181.9B**, revised from **$188.6B**, which suggests the market is still positive but slightly less aggressive than before.

**Reliability assessment:** strong. This dimension is supported by broad public proxy coverage, though full institutional detail remains behind paywalls.  
**Evidence gap:** segment-level low/median/high tables for FY2026 are not fully visible in free sources.

## 2) KPI Focus

The market is focused on a narrow set of operating indicators that best explain NVIDIA’s valuation:

1. **Revenue growth**, especially total company and Data Center
2. **EPS and operating leverage**
3. **Free cash flow generation**
4. **Gross margin sustainability**
5. **Networking attach / full-system sales**, not just GPU units

The consensus framework assumes that Data Center continues to drive the bulk of incremental revenue, supported by Blackwell/B-series ramp, GPU supply normalization, and expanded system-level sales. Margin expectations remain strong because the market believes NVIDIA can sustain premium economics through software, CUDA, networking, and full-stack system content.

The key risk here is not whether NVIDIA can deliver strong numbers this year, but whether the mix of product ramp costs, pricing discipline, and competitive intensity leads to margin compression over time. The market still expects **mid-70% gross margins**, which is very strong for a company scaling so quickly.

**Reliability assessment:** strong. These are the metrics most consistently discussed by analysts and company guidance.  
**Evidence gap:** free cash flow and segment gross margin estimates are less available in public consensus tables than revenue/EPS.

## 3) Pricing Assumptions

Valuation is still the most debated part of the consensus story.

One framework implies **strong multi-year EBITDA growth**, with forward EBITDA multiple compression from roughly **34.6x current** to about **17.1x Jan. 2027** and **11.9x Jan. 2028**. Another framework shows **EV/forward EBITDA around 19.4x**, versus a **five-year average of 12.6x**. Both views point to the same conclusion: the stock is expensive on today’s earnings, but the multiple can normalize quickly if growth stays exceptional.

Relative to peers, NVIDIA still trades at a discount in some forward P/E tables:
- **AVGO forward P/E:** **63.6x**
- **AMD forward P/E:** **177.7x**
- **MRVL forward P/E:** **107.5x**

This is important because it shows how stretched the rest of the semiconductor AI complex has become. NVIDIA is still expensive in absolute terms, but the market is paying less of a premium for NVDA than for several AI-linked peers on forward earnings.

**Reliability assessment:** strong, but mixed across frameworks because different models use different forward periods and EBITDA assumptions.  
**Evidence gap:** the exact base case for terminal margins and post-ramp growth is not observable from public consensus alone.
